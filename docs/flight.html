Flying

Takeoff, wheeling, banking, coming back down. No bird ever looks at another bird, which is what makes the cost flat in flock size.

Takeoff

Committed: the launch direction is chosen once, from Takeoff Up Bias blended with away-from-threat, then eased to Takeoff Speed over Takeoff Time rather than snapping.

PropertyDefault
Takeoff Time0.35show long the launch lasts. Not the length of a clip
Takeoff Speed600
Takeoff Up Bias1how much of the launch is straight up
Takeoff Away Bias0.6how much is directly away from whatever spooked it
Min Flight Time2sshortest time airborne before a bird will consider coming down

A Take Off clip that ends before the launch does hands over to Take Off Loop, or to Fly. One that runs longer than the launch plays out in full: a one-shot is never cut mid-pose.

Take Off Loop only ever plays if Takeoff Time outlasts the Take Off clip, and the default 0.35s launch is shorter than most takeoff animations. If yours matters, raise Takeoff Time past the clip's length.

Wheeling

Airborne birds steer toward one attractor per flock that sweeps around it. That one moving point is what makes a flock wheel.

PropertyDefault
Cruise Radius1200how wide the circuit is
Cruise Ceiling900how high above the flock
Cruise Speed500
Attractor Sweep Degrees70/show fast the attractor goes round, which is the wheeling speed
Turn Rate Degrees160/shardest turn a bird can make
Cohesion Weight1how hard birds are pulled toward the attractor
Jitter Amplitude180per-bird wander, against that pull
Jitter Frequency1.7
Orbit Time Min / Max3-9show long a circling bird stays up
Orbit Preference0.5chance a spooked bird wheels rather than heading straight back down. The volume can override it
The circuit is invisible unless the volume is selected, and the usual surprise is that it reaches through a roof or a cliff the volume itself sits clear of. Birds have no collision, so nothing stops them. Look at it once per flock. See Placing a flock.

Separation

The one exception to no bird looks at another bird. Airborne birds inside Separation Radius of a flockmate push apart.

PropertyDefault
Enable Separationon
Separation Max TierNearruns at this tier and every closer one
Separation Radius90
Separation Strength400

It is O(n²) within a chunk, so the further out it is allowed to run the more it costs and the less anyone can see it. It is chunk-local rather than grid-based: a flock's shared-fragment value already partitions its own chunks, so nothing spatial is rebuilt each frame.

flock.Separation 0 forces it off, 1 forces it on at every tier, -1 (default) leaves it to the species. A cheat, and stripped from Shipping: the species setting is what ships.

Banking

Birds roll into their turns by the angle it would actually take to hold that turn at their speed, eased in at Bank Interp Speed and capped at Bank Scale. So a wide slow orbit leans a little and a hard break lays right over, with neither tuned for, and the angle is the same at any frame rate.

PropertyDefault
Bank Scale35°a ceiling, not the angle. Lower it to keep birds level, raise it to let a hard turn lay right over. 0 never rolls
Bank Interp Speed6how fast roll reaches what the turn asks for
Bank Clip Yaw Rate60°/sturning harder than this swaps Fly for the bank clip. Capped by Turn Rate Degrees, so a value at or above that never triggers
Glide Descent Rate60descending faster than this swaps Fly for Glide. 0 never glides

A bird only banks from turning. Going down is not turning, and a descent steep enough to have no heading at all holds the heading it had rather than deriving a meaningless one from a near-vertical velocity.

If a bank clip is only the fly clip with a static roll on it, do not author it. The roll above is continuous, proportional and eases; a clip's is a fixed angle that snaps on and off, and cannot ease because it loops. Author Bank Left/Right for a genuinely different pose - wings swept, tail fanned - and set Bank Scale to 0 if you do, so the two do not stack.

Clip choice in the air: Bank Left/Right past Bank Clip Yaw Rate, else Glide past Glide Descent Rate, else Fly. A clip that has started holds until well under the threshold that started it, so nothing chatters at the boundary. Both alternatives are optional and Fly covers whatever is unmapped, so a species with neither never changes clip in the air.

Coming down

A bird heading straight back looks for a slot at least Safe Relocate Distance from what spooked it and on the far side of itself, within Land Search Radius. Failing that it orbits instead.

PropertyDefault
Land Search Radius3000how far a bird will look for somewhere to settle
Safe Relocate Distance800a relocating bird will not settle within this of whatever spooked it
Land Approach Height250height above the target it lines up at before dropping
Land Speed350
Land Arrive Distance30how close to directly above counts as lined up, at twice this
Land Velocity Interp Speed2.5how quickly a bird that has committed swings onto its descent. Low is a wide unhurried turn; high is abrupt
Max Orbit Time20swith no slot free for this long, a bird gives up and drops onto open ground where it started

Committing eases rather than snaps: both the speed and the direction change through Land Velocity Interp Speed, the turn onto the approach is limited by Turn Rate Degrees like any other, and the bird arrives on the frame it would otherwise overshoot, so it is never placed onto the spot from a distance.

The descent plays Land Loop; Land is the arrival and plays once the feet are down. See Clips.

A descent is only ever placed, never steered. It is aimed at an authored spot, so turning a bird away from that spot is a bird that hovers beside a blocking volume instead of arriving. The placement stops too once the bird is within Land Arrive Distance, so a volume clipping the spot cannot eject it every frame forever.

Then it stays down

Landed Cooldown keeps it from bouncing straight back up: alert decays Refractory Decay Scale faster and the flee bar is raised by Refractory Flee Bonus while it lasts. See Being noticed.
