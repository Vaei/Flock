Reactive flocks

Birds the player reads. Their leaving is information, so it has to be legible from a distance and it has to mean something specific.

Five moves

  1. Put them where they can be seen leaving. Against sky, over a wall, above a rooftop. A flock scattering into foliage tells nobody anything.
  2. Make the break sharp. Proximity Exponent 2, tighter Threshold Jitter, higher Contagion Strength. A reactive flock should go in under a second, visibly.
  3. Make them relocate. Orbit Preference 0.15. A flock that comes straight back has un-told the player what it told them.
  4. Fire the scare from the animation, not from gameplay code. Where in a swing the noise happens is a property of the animation.
  5. Give it sound. A scatter nobody hears is a scatter half the players miss.

Ambient Airborne Fraction goes down, which is the opposite of an ambient flock. A bird already circling is one the player cannot tell apart from a bird that just left, and the whole point here is that leaving is the signal.

Takeoff Up Bias above 1 is what makes a scatter read from ground level. The launch direction is that bias blended with directly-away-from-threat; weighted toward up, the flock breaks the skyline instead of spreading sideways behind whatever the player is standing next to.

Firing it deliberately

A scare is a source with a lifetime, not a command. For Duration it sits in the same list a walking player does and is felt through the same falloff, so birds in the middle break and scatter while the ones at the edge only look round, and none of them do it on the exact frame of the bang.

From an animation

Add a Scare Flock notify to the montage - a swing, a landing, a shout - and set Socket Name so it centres on the blade rather than the middle of the character. This is the preferred way in, and it no-ops in preview and editor worlds.

From code

#include "System/FlockStatics.h"

UFlockStatics::ScareFlock(this, GetActorLocation(), /*Radius*/ 2000.f);

Nothing to register, nothing to clean up. ScareFlockAtActor is the same centred on an actor. Both are BlueprintCallable.

Sources other than the player

Every APawn already alarms birds, at Auto Source Threat Weight 1 and Auto Source Radius 1200. Beyond that, three ways in:

Flock Disturbance Componenta rolling boulder, a cat, a thrown object. Threat Weight, Max Radius, and SetDisturbanceEnabled for a source that comes and goes. Always outranks the class list
IFlockDisturbanceInterfacea type your own module owns. IsFlockThreatActive is polled every frame, which is where unnoticed while crouched lives
FlockIgnore actor tagopts one actor out of the auto-registered classes entirely
IsFlockThreatActive is the hook for stealth. Return false while the player is crouched, in cover, or unseen, and the birds stop being a tell - which makes them a tell again the moment the player stands up. This costs nothing: only actors that actually implement the interface are polled.

What it should cost

A reactive flock is looked at, close, at the moment it matters, so it is the one that can justify spending.

Separation on at Nearthe default. Birds passing through each other during a scatter is exactly when someone is watching
Interpolate Frames onif the launch is seen up close. Both halves are needed: the species setting and Interpolate on the material instance. See Frame interpolation
Two bone influencespays for the interpolation outright on a small skeleton. Try it before deciding interpolation is too expensive
Take Off VFXone batched Niagara component per flock, not one per bird. Dust off a ledge sells a launch cheaply

Checklist

  • The flock is against something they can be seen leaving.
  • The whole break happens inside a second, and not on one frame.
  • They relocate rather than coming back.
  • The scare comes off the animation, on the right frame.
  • You can hear it happen with your eyes shut.
  • Nothing scares them that should not.
